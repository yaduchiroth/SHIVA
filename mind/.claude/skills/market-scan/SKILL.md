# Market scan

A scan is not a list of links. It is a map someone can act on.

## Steps
1. **Frame it.** Write the question in one line: what market, what boundary, what
   decision it informs. If the boundary is fuzzy, pick one and say you picked it.
2. **Sweep.** Search 4–8 angles, not one: the category name, the job-to-be-done
   phrasing, the two obvious incumbents, pricing pages, recent funding, and what
   users complain about. Different phrasings surface different players.
3. **Sort.** Group what you find into 3–5 clusters by *approach*, not by size.
   Two companies solving it the same way belong together.
4. **Price it.** Find real numbers where you can. "Pricing not public" is a
   finding — say it rather than skipping the row.
5. **Find the gap.** Name what nobody in the set is doing well. That's usually
   the only part of the scan that changes a decision.

## Deliver
- One line: the state of the market.
- A table on the HUD (`hud_report`): player · approach · pricing · notable.
- Three bullets: where the gap is, who's closest to it, what would beat them.
- Sources with dates. Anything older than 18 months gets marked stale.

## Never
Never present the count of companies as the insight. Never pad the table with
players you couldn't verify. Never let a marketing claim in without attribution.
